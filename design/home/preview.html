Design proposal

The home page shows five of 479 levels, and gives a quarter of itself to the staff roster

Home was rebuilt once already, and that pass fixed the biggest thing wrong with it: the landing page of a level list now shows levels. What it did not fix is the geometry those levels sit on, and the weight the page gives each block. The hero and the body are on two different columns; the status pill on a row sits a thousand pixels from the name it belongs to; the two progress numbers this whole site is built on are written as words rather than drawn; and the change feed is a scroll box inside a page that scrolls.

These four templates are two ways to answer that on each surface. A and C keep every block the page has and fix what carries it. B and D ask what a visitor comes to a tracker for and rebuild home around the three answers. Each pair is one idea on two surfaces, so a decision is A+C or B+D, not four separate calls.

Constraint
No new fields, no new routes, no per-level prose. Every figure shown is one the app already computes.
Reused
css/ull-v2.css and css/pages/mobile-v2.css as they ship: hero, pill, meter, row, card, eyebrow, button, credentials bar.
New
Layout only — one column measure, a row modifier, a spotlight wrapper, a feed in flow. No new card, pill or heading.
Not covered
The footer, the sidebar and the mobile shell. All four templates keep them exactly as they are.
Measured on the shipped page — Chromium, 1440×900 and 390×844, with the live list

A. Spotlight

desktop · A
pages/a-spotlight.html · js/pages/Home.js · css/pages/home.css

Keep every block the page has and fix the geometry it sits on. One column carries the hero, the credentials, the rows and the feed, so the page has a single left edge instead of the two it had — 72px apart at 1440, 312px apart at 1920. The half of the hero that was empty at every width goes to #1, drawn the way /level/<slug> draws it, with its blurred thumbnail, its status pill and both progress meters, and the copy fills the column beside it. Under it, not the next five rows of the list but the five levels above the hardest verified level that have got furthest through verification — the ones that would take the Top 1 spot if they were finished today, in the order they are likely to do it. The feed keeps its scroll window, and the editors card becomes a line of names.

What it fixes

The first screen stops being an advert and starts being the list. A visitor sees one level in full — where it stands, how far the decoration got, how far anyone has got through it — plus four more with the same numbers drawn rather than written. The staff roster drops from 465px of card to one wrapped line, and every row on the page — the spotlight, the credentials, the lane, the feed — is drawn from one left edge and one right edge.

What it costs to build

Cheapest of the two desktop templates. .home-hero, .home-content and .home-page .u-cred collapse into one .hm-col rule; the spotlight is .u-hero + .u-meter, both already shipped, fed by the level the page already has in memory at index 0; the row gains .u-bar--thin, also shipped. No new data and no new route. The editors line needs the same fetchEditors call it makes today.

What to watch for

The spotlight is only ever #1, so the page leads with a level that may sit at 100% decoration for months — it is the top of the list, not news. The Top 1 lane is ordered by progress rather than by placement, so its rank column does not count down, which reads as a mistake until the note under the heading is read.

B. Board

desktop · B
pages/b-board.html · js/pages/Home.js · css/pages/home.css

Ask what a visitor comes to a tracker's landing page to find out, and answer all of it above the fold. The hero shrinks to a band — one line of what this is, one filled button — and the page becomes three lanes on one grid: the top of the list, the levels closest to being verified, and what the staff changed this week. The middle lane is the top of /upcoming in that page's own order, so it is a reading the site already computes rather than a new one. The staff roster leaves the body and becomes a credit line above the footer, where the same twelve names already sit on Information.

What it fixes

The empty right half of the hero is gone because the hero is no longer a half-width block on a full-width band, and the page uses the width the window gives it at 1920 rather than a 1100px column with 312px of dead margin beside a full-bleed hero. Three questions get one screen each in a row instead of one question getting the page and the other two being buried below a staff card.

What it costs to build

The largest of the four. Home gains a second reading of the list — upcomingRanking() from js/formulas.js, which /upcoming already calls, sliced to six — and the lane grid replaces .home-content's single column. The feed leaves its nested window. Still no new field, no new route, and no per-level prose.

What to watch for

It reopens a decision this repo already made once: the five-count strip was cut because home is not a dashboard. Three lanes of levels is not five counts of one list, but it is denser, and a first-time visitor now meets the project in one line rather than three. If the hero is the thing that has to convince somebody who has never heard of ULL, A holds that better than B does.

C. Deck

phone · C
pages/c-deck.html · js/pages/mobile/MobileHome.js · css/pages/mobile-v2.css

The desktop template A, at 390px. The hero keeps its eyebrow and title but loses two of its four lines of lead, so the screen a visitor lands on opens on a level instead of on an advert: the credentials bar, then #1 with its blurred thumbnail, its status pill and both meters, then the next five as rows carrying their status beside the name and their decoration as a bar. Recent Changes keeps the bounded window it scrolls inside, and the editors keep the full card they have, grouped by role, at the foot of the page.

What it fixes

321px of the 792px window — 41% — used to go by before the first level, and what appeared was a name in a row. Here the first level starts at roughly 320px and is shown in full: where it stands, how far the decoration got and how far anyone has got through it. And the lane under it is the desktop's, so the two surfaces show the same section under the spotlight.

What it costs to build

The same shape as the desktop template, so both can be built from one pass. The spotlight is .u-hero plus two .u-meter, all shipped; the row modifier is a .u-bar--thin under the existing .m2-row body; the lane is the same slice of the same list the desktop takes.

What to watch for

The spotlight costs a full thumbnail load on the first paint of the phone's landing page, and it pushes the row list further down than the credentials bar above it does. And the lane is ordered by progress rather than by placement, so its rank column does not count down, which reads as a mistake until the note under the heading is read.

D. Segments

phone · D
pages/d-segments.html · js/pages/mobile/MobileHome.js · css/pages/mobile-v2.css

The desktop template B, at 390px, where three lanes cannot sit side by side. The hero shrinks to two lines and a credentials row, and under it a segment bar that sticks to the top of the scroll — Top of the list, Closest to verified, What changed — with one lane on screen at a time. Nothing is two screens down any more: every one of the three answers is one tap from the landing screen, and each lane is its own scroll from its own top. The editors become a credit row at the foot of every lane.

What it fixes

The page is 2062px of scroll today, and its second and third blocks — a feed nobody can reach past a nested scroll box, and a 499px staff roster — are the bottom two thirds of it. Segments make the page one screen deep in every direction. The middle lane is new to the phone: the levels closest to being verified is the reading /mobile/upcoming already orders by, and it is the one thing on this site that changes week to week.

What it costs to build

A segment bar is state on the component plus the shipped .m2-tab styling; the lanes are the rows the page already renders. It needs mobileStore.rawList, which the phone already holds, and upcomingRanking() from js/formulas.js, which /mobile/upcoming already calls. The bar is the same pattern the /mobile/info deck proposed as its Shelf template, so the phone would have one segmented page and one hub page.

What to watch for

Segments hide two thirds of the page behind a tap, and a visitor who does not notice the bar sees only the top six levels — the same trap the old "Other Pages ▼" accordion set, one level up. It also splits what is one scroll on the desktop into three on the phone, so the two surfaces stop matching block for block, which every template in design/mobile/ was careful to keep.
