Design proposal

The last page on the phone that is still on the old styling

The desktop /information has been rebuilt as a hub: a hero that says what the list is, one search field over everything, and six faces that open a reader — the FAQ, the site map, the guidelines, the two legends, the staff and contact routing, and the API. The phone still shows the page it showed before that: an editor list, two legends written out as sentences, and the guidelines in a scroll box inside a scrolling page. It is missing four of the six blocks entirely.

These are three ways to put the same content on a 390px screen. All three carry everything the desktop page has and nothing it does not; what differs is where the long reads live — behind a door, on a shelf, or printed in one document.

A. Doors

the desktop hub, stacked
pages/a-doors.html · js/pages/mobile/MobileInfo.js · css/pages/mobile-info.css

The desktop page, turned on its side. The hero says what the list is and offers the full version at the end of the sentence, the search field sits under it, and the six faces run down the screen in one column in the desktop's own order — FAQ, the site map, the guidelines, the reference, the staff, the API. Each face is still a face: a label, a name, one sentence and the size of what is behind it. Tapping one raises a reader over the whole screen, tab bar included, with its own bar and a close button where the thumb is. The guidelines reader opens on its fourteen sections and pushes one section over the index, which is what the desktop's index-beside-body split becomes when there is no beside.

How it settles the weighting

The same way the desktop settles it, minus the column spans: order. The FAQ is first because it is what most visitors arrive wanting, and the guidelines face keeps its five group counts because sizing up the rulebook without opening it is the one thing that block has to do. Nothing long is ever on the page — one screen and a half of faces, then everything else is one tap deep.

What it costs to build

Lowest of the three. MobileInfo.js becomes the same component as Information.js with one column and a sheet instead of a centred window: the same ?open= keys, the same seven bodies, the same search index. The phone layer gains a full-screen sheet and the reading kit; nothing else is new.

What to watch for

Everything is behind a tap, so the counts on the faces are doing real work and the search has to reach inside closed readers or it is decoration. The guidelines are two taps from the page (open, then pick a section) where the desktop is one, and Back has to unwind that in the right order — section, then index, then page. The reader covers the tab bar, so its close button is the only way out and it must be reachable without a scroll.

Information

The Upcoming Levels List forecasts which Extreme Demons are about to be verified and placed on the Demonlist. This page says how that is decided, what everything on the site means, who to ask, and how to read the whole list as JSON.

Start hereWhat this list is

The Upcoming Levels List catalogues upcoming Top 1–100 Extreme Demons projected to be verified and placed on the Demonlist, along with unrated Extreme Demons that would have qualified for a rating when they were made. It is a forecast of what the Demonlist is about to look like — not a record of what has already happened.

Nothing here is official. Positions are estimates made by the list staff against the criteria written in the guidelines, and they move as levels progress. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate.

The three tiers

The order of levels is the same in all three. What changes is the threshold to appear at all.

  • All Levels — every level with a conceivable chance of being verified and published. A level’s rank here is what leaderboard points are calculated from.
  • Main List — levels that meet the standards required to be considered for an official rating.
  • Future List — levels very likely to be verified and published soon.

How to read a level

The colour of a name is the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. Two percentages follow every level — how much of the decoration is done, and how far the best run has got. The badges are its position in each tier: a level can be #4 in All Levels and #2 in Future List at once.

Answers · 15 questionsFAQ
Getting on the list
How does a level get on the list?

Staff select it. There is no queue a creator joins: moderators assess levels against the Level Selection criteria in the guidelines — classic gameplay, a public recording, an intended release on the official servers and a credible chance of being rated — and place the ones that pass on the Pending List until an exact position is settled.

My level isn’t listed. Can I put it forward?

Raise it in #list-discussion on the Discord server with a public recording of its current state. It is not a submission form and the staff are not obliged to add it; it goes through the same criteria as everything else.

Why is a level on Pending and not on the list?

It passed selection but has no exact position yet. The Pending List shows the range it is expected to land in and an arrow for which way it is moving inside that range. A level with no usable estimate at all sits under Pending Indefinitely.

A level’s information is wrong or out of date.

Report it in #level-update-reporting with something to back it up — gameplay footage, a statement from the creator, tester feedback. Positions are adjusted on that kind of evidence.

Why is a level marked 🚫?

It is pending removal: it no longer meets the criteria it was added under, and will come off the list unless that changes.

Records
How do I submit a record?

Through the list’s Discord server. Read Acceptance of Records in the guidelines first — a record that arrives without the required proof is rejected before anyone assesses it, not sent back for more.

What proof does a record need?

A complete, uncut playthrough of the record (if your video has cuts, attach the raw footage as well), the level’s audio or your clicks, and a cheat indicator and fps/tps display where your mod menu provides them. The record has to be on the version of the level this site lists. The full requirements, including what may be blurred and what may not, are in Requirements for proof of legitimacy.

What counts as a world record here?

Two things are tracked separately: the best completion from 0%, and the world record run — the longest single segment on the current version of the level, measured from where it started to where it ended.

My record was rejected. Can it be reviewed?

A record rejected because the player changed the level can be reviewed if the creator later made the same change in a new version; ask the staff. Anything rejected for missing proof needs to be resubmitted with the proof.

Points and the leaderboard
How are leaderboard points calculated?

From two things: the level’s rank in All Levels, and the percentage of your record. A verification is worth twice a 100% record on the same level, and completing a level that is not verified yet — a layout completion — is worth 0.8 of a verification.

#1#10#50#100
100% record14591196663425
50% record851698387248
Verification291923921325849

Position is worth far more than percentage: a 50% on #1 beats a 100% on #100.

Why did my total change when I didn’t submit anything?

Because points are calculated from a level’s current rank, and on a list of upcoming levels ranks move constantly. Every record on a level that moves is worth a different number of points afterwards.

What is “layout verified”?

A 100% completion of a level that has not been verified yet — someone beat it in its undecorated state. It is scored as its own thing, at 0.8 of a verification.

The list itself
What do the colours in the list mean?

They are the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. The full scale is in the Reference block on this page, and it is the same scale as the status pill on a level’s own page. Level colouring is a setting — if names look plain, turn it on in Settings.

Is this the Demonlist? Is it official?

No. ULL is a community project and is not affiliated with RobTop Games. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate, and these guidelines are adapted from the Global Demonlist Guidelines with credit to their authors — but nothing here is an official ranking.

The rules changed and I didn’t know.

Changes are announced in the Discord server. The guidelines on this page are always the current version.

Build on it · 9 endpointsAPI documentation

Everything this site shows comes from a public JSON API. It needs no key, no signup and no referrer, and it sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, so a page or a bot can read the list straight from the browser.

Base URL

https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev

Endpoints

GET/api/listEvery level, in rank order
GET/api/list/mainThe Main List
GET/api/list/futureThe Future List
GET/api/levels/{position}One level, by its 1-based rank
GET/api/pendingPending List entries
GET/api/editorsThe staff list, in the order staff arranged it
GET/api/level-monthLevel of the Month, or null
GET/api/level-verifClosest to verification, or null
GET/api/recent-changesThe changes feed, grouped {date, entries[]}

Writing to the list needs a staff API key and is not part of the public API. There is no public leaderboard endpoint: the leaderboard is derived from /api/list, by the rules written out under “How are leaderboard points calculated?” in the FAQ.

Example

curl https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev/api/list [ { "path": "manray", "name": "M A N R A Y", "author": "akunakunn", "creators": ["akunakunn", "Wobbly"], "verifier": "Open Verification", "percentFinished": 80, "records": [{ "user": "Zoink", "percent": 61, "hz": 240 }], "isMain": true, "isFuture": false, "isVerified": false, "sort_order": 4 }, … ]

The level object

path stringSlug; the level’s page is /level/{path}
name, author stringLevel name and host
creators string[]Everyone credited
verifier stringVerifier, or “Open Verification”
percentFinished numberDecoration progress, 0–100
records, run object[]{user, link, percent, hz} — completions and runs
tags string[]Public, Layout, Rated, NONG, …
isMain, isFuture, isVerified, benchmark booleanWhich tiers it is on, and its state
sort_order numberRank in All Levels

Fair use

  • Cache what you fetch: the list changes a few times a day, not a few times a second.
  • Identify your bot in the user agent if you are polling on a schedule.
  • The data is community work — credit the list and link back to it.
  • Positions are estimates and change, so treat a stored rank as a snapshot, not a fact.

B. Shelf

one sticky segment bar, no overlays
pages/b-shelf.html · js/pages/mobile/MobileInfo.js · css/pages/mobile-info.css

The page is a shelf of seven parts and you are always standing in one of them. A compact hero, the search under it, then a sticky segment bar that scrolls sideways — Start, FAQ, Pages, Rules, Marks, Staff, API — and under it the part itself, printed in the page. Nothing opens over anything: there is no scrim, no scroll lock, no focus trap, and Back does what Back does. The Rules segment carries a second row for the five guideline groups, so the fourteen sections are two taps deep at most and a whole group reads as one scroll, in order, the way a rulebook is meant to be read.

How it settles the weighting

The bar itself. Seven segments in the desktop's order, with the first one — Start — holding the page's own definition of the list, so a first-time visitor lands on the answer rather than on six doors. Every segment carries the eyebrow, name and sentence the desktop face has, so nothing is lost by the faces going away; what goes is the summary screen that showed all six at once.

What it costs to build

Middling. No modal component at all, which is the largest thing the other two need — the panels are v-if on the same ?open= key. New is the sticky segment bar, its overflow, and remembering which group of the guidelines you were in. The search opens a segment instead of a window, which is one method rather than two.

What to watch for

You can no longer see the whole page at once: there is no screen that shows all six blocks together, so the bar has to name them well and it has to be obvious that it scrolls past API. Two sticky rows above the content on the Rules segment eat 6rem of a 844px screen. The desktop's previous/next pair through the fourteen sections becomes group chips plus scrolling, so check that reading straight through still works.

General

Glossary

The terms used throughout these guidelines and across the site.

A level’s state

  • Layout — a level whose gameplay exists but which has no decoration yet.
  • Decoration progress — how much of the level’s decoration is finished, as a percentage. It is what colours a level’s name up to the point decoration is complete.
  • Verification progress — how far anyone has got on the current version of the level, taken from the best record or the longest run. It takes over the name colour once decoration is finished.
  • Verified — the level has been completed in full and is no longer upcoming.
  • Rated / Unrated — whether the level has received an official in-game rating from the developer. A verified level can sit here unrated for some time.
  • Pending — the level has passed selection but has no exact position yet; the Pending List shows the range it is expected to land in. Pending Indefinitely means not even a range can be estimated yet.

Records

  • World record (from 0%) — the highest completion percentage achieved from the start of the level. World record run — the longest single segment on the current version, measured between where it started and where it ended.
  • Verifier — the player who will verify the level. Open Verification means no verifier has been decided.
  • Layout completion — a 100% completion of a level that is not verified yet, i.e. beaten in its undecorated state. It is scored separately from an ordinary record.

On the list

  • All Levels, Main List, Future List — the three tiers. The order is the same in all three; the threshold to appear at all is what differs.
  • Benchmark — a verified level kept on the list as a reference point for difficulty. Benchmark Mode, in Settings, hides every other verified level and renumbers what is left.
  • Remake — a new version of a level that already exists.
  • NONG / NC — the level’s music is not hosted on Newgrounds, or is not copyright-free.
  • Medium, Long, XL, XXL, XXXL — how long the gameplay runs.

What this list is

The Upcoming Levels List catalogues upcoming Top 1–100 Extreme Demons projected to be verified and placed on the Demonlist, along with unrated Extreme Demons that would have qualified for a rating when they were made. It is a forecast of what the Demonlist is about to look like — not a record of what has already happened.

Nothing here is official. Positions are estimates made by the list staff against the criteria written in the guidelines, and they move as levels progress. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate.

The three tiers

The order of levels is the same in all three. What changes is the threshold to appear at all.

  • All Levels — every level with a conceivable chance of being verified and published. A level’s rank here is what leaderboard points are calculated from.
  • Main List — levels that meet the standards required to be considered for an official rating.
  • Future List — levels very likely to be verified and published soon.

How to read a level

The colour of a name is the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. Two percentages follow every level — how much of the decoration is done, and how far the best run has got. The badges are its position in each tier: a level can be #4 in All Levels and #2 in Future List at once.

Answers · 15 questions

FAQ

Submitting a record and what proof it needs, how a level gets listed, how leaderboard points are worked out, and why a total changes when you have submitted nothing. 15 questions in 4 groups, each answered here rather than pointed at the guidelines.

Getting on the list 5
How does a level get on the list?

Staff select it. There is no queue a creator joins: moderators assess levels against the Level Selection criteria in the guidelines — classic gameplay, a public recording, an intended release on the official servers and a credible chance of being rated — and place the ones that pass on the Pending List until an exact position is settled.

My level isn’t listed. Can I put it forward?

Raise it in #list-discussion on the Discord server with a public recording of its current state. It is not a submission form and the staff are not obliged to add it; it goes through the same criteria as everything else.

Why is a level on Pending and not on the list?

It passed selection but has no exact position yet. The Pending List shows the range it is expected to land in and an arrow for which way it is moving inside that range. A level with no usable estimate at all sits under Pending Indefinitely.

A level’s information is wrong or out of date.

Report it in #level-update-reporting with something to back it up — gameplay footage, a statement from the creator, tester feedback. Positions are adjusted on that kind of evidence.

Why is a level marked 🚫?

It is pending removal: it no longer meets the criteria it was added under, and will come off the list unless that changes.

Records 4
How do I submit a record?

Through the list’s Discord server. Read Acceptance of Records in the guidelines first — a record that arrives without the required proof is rejected before anyone assesses it, not sent back for more.

What proof does a record need?

A complete, uncut playthrough of the record (if your video has cuts, attach the raw footage as well), the level’s audio or your clicks, and a cheat indicator and fps/tps display where your mod menu provides them. The record has to be on the version of the level this site lists. The full requirements, including what may be blurred and what may not, are in Requirements for proof of legitimacy.

What counts as a world record here?

Two things are tracked separately: the best completion from 0%, and the world record run — the longest single segment on the current version of the level, measured from where it started to where it ended.

My record was rejected. Can it be reviewed?

A record rejected because the player changed the level can be reviewed if the creator later made the same change in a new version; ask the staff. Anything rejected for missing proof needs to be resubmitted with the proof.

Points and the leaderboard 3
How are leaderboard points calculated?

From two things: the level’s rank in All Levels, and the percentage of your record. A verification is worth twice a 100% record on the same level, and completing a level that is not verified yet — a layout completion — is worth 0.8 of a verification.

#1#10#50#100
100% record14591196663425
50% record851698387248
Verification291923921325849

Position is worth far more than percentage: a 50% on #1 beats a 100% on #100.

Why did my total change when I didn’t submit anything?

Because points are calculated from a level’s current rank, and on a list of upcoming levels ranks move constantly. Every record on a level that moves is worth a different number of points afterwards.

What is “layout verified”?

A 100% completion of a level that has not been verified yet — someone beat it in its undecorated state. It is scored as its own thing, at 0.8 of a verification.

The list itself 3
What do the colours in the list mean?

They are the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. The full scale is under What the marks mean on this page, and it is the same scale as the status pill on a level’s own page. Level colouring is a setting — if names look plain, turn it on in Settings.

Is this the Demonlist? Is it official?

No. ULL is a community project and is not affiliated with RobTop Games. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate, and these guidelines are adapted from the Global Demonlist Guidelines with credit to their authors — but nothing here is an official ranking.

The rules changed and I didn’t know.

Changes are announced in the Discord server. The guidelines on this page are always the current version.
